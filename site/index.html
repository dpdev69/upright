Free and open source · macOS 13 or later

Your Logitech mouse, without Logi Options+.

Upright is a small menu-bar app that remaps the buttons on Logitech mice. It talks to the mouse directly over HID++, so there's no account, no background daemon and no kernel extension.

In front

Everything Logi Options+ did for your buttons, in one small native app.

Upright lives in the menu bar, looks like part of macOS, and only does what you set up.

Buttons

Give every button a job.

Back, Forward, the top button, the wheel click and any other button your mouse lets software take over. Map each one to Mission Control, a keyboard shortcut, an app, a URL or a Shortcuts.app shortcut.

  • Mission Control
  • App Exposé
  • Show Desktop
  • Spaces
  • Media keys
  • Any shortcut
  • Open App
  • Run Shortcut

Gestures

One button, five actions.

Set a button to Gestures and it gets a click, plus hold-and-move up, down, left or right. The mouse reports raw movement while you hold the button, so the cursor stays where it is.

Click → Mission Control · Up → Show Desktop · Down → App Exposé · Left and right → switch Spaces

Per-app profiles

Different buttons for different apps.

Keep one global setup and let apps override single buttons while they're in front. In Safari the thumb buttons can switch tabs. Everywhere else they go back and forward.

Several mice

Every mouse you own, at once.

USB cable, Bluetooth, Unifying, Bolt or Nano, in any mix. Each mouse gets its own row with its connection and battery, and a mouse that's asleep never holds up the others.

Low-battery alerts at 15% and 5%, for each mouse.

Pointer speed

Pick a speed. Keep it.

Set the DPI with a slider, save presets, and map a button to cycle through them. Upright applies your speed again whenever the mouse reconnects, rounded to what that mouse supports.

Mice with ADJUSTABLE_DPI (0x2201)

Scrolling

Set the wheel apart from the trackpad.

Reverse the mouse wheel without touching the trackpad's Natural Scrolling, change the speed from 0.5× to 3×, or try the experimental smooth scrolling. All of it is off until you turn it on.

Leaves nothing behind.

Nothing written to the mouse

Buttons are only diverted while Upright runs. Quit it, or switch the mouse off and on, and every button is back to factory behavior.

No drivers, no daemon

One native SwiftUI app in your menu bar. No kernel extension, no background service, no Logitech software and no account.

Never reads your typing

Upright only opens Logitech HID interfaces and only sends the actions you assign. Keyboards are never opened.

HID++ 2.0 over USB, Bluetooth and receivers 0x1B04 REPROG_CONTROLS_V4 0x2201 ADJUSTABLE_DPI Written from public protocol documentation only

Questions

Is Upright free?

Yes. It's open source under the MIT license, with no account and no subscription.

Which mice work?

The MX Vertical is verified on real hardware. Other Logitech mice that speak HID++ 2.0 and have programmable buttons are expected to work, including the MX Master, MX Anywhere, MX Ergo and Lift families, but they haven't been verified on hardware yet. Upright decides by what a mouse answers, not by its name, so mice outside the catalog can work too.

macOS won't open Upright the first time. What do I do?

A build that isn't notarized is blocked by Gatekeeper on first launch. Right-click Upright.app, choose Open, then Open again. You only need to do this once.

Do I need to remove Logi Options+?

Quit it and take it out of your login items while you use Upright. Logi Options+, Solaar and LogiOps divert the same buttons, and only one app can have them.

Does it change my mouse permanently?

No. Nothing is written to the mouse's memory. Quit Upright and your buttons go back to normal straight away.

What permissions does it ask for?

Accessibility, to send the shortcuts, clicks and media keys your buttons are mapped to. Input Monitoring only if macOS hides a mouse over Bluetooth. Mission Control actions, opening apps and pointer speed work without either.

Can I build it myself?

Yes. Open Upright.xcodeproj in Xcode 15.3 or later and press ⌘R. You don't need an Apple Developer account.
